Traceable, transparent rules for public benefits

The Axiom Foundation is one of eleven projects in the Public Benefit Innovation Fund’s Spring 2026 cohort, announced on October 22. We will work with a state agency to test open encodings of SNAP (food assistance) eligibility rules — rules written as software that people can read and computers can run, with every provision linked to the law it comes from.

The transparently encoded rules become a foundation states can build on: government systems give families faster, more accurate decisions, and the same rules can ground the AI tools states are adopting. We will publish the encodings and what we learn under an open license.

Benchmark snapshot
GPT-6 Sol takes the lead
PolicyBench is an open benchmark from the Axiom Foundation and PolicyEngine, scoring 50 models on United States household tax and benefit calculations. GPT-6 Sol leads at 95.2 percent of sampled households answered to within a dollar, and every model in the top ten now clears 90 percent, where a month ago only the leader cleared 88. The board also turned on itself this autumn: we audited the answer key against the law, using the models’ misses to find bugs in our own reference engine. See the full board →

product
Product updates

New app interface

The Axiom App now opens on a map, with every state shaded by how many provisions we have encoded there and a chip above it for federal law. The jurisdictions too small to shade get their own labels, so Washington, D.C. and Rhode Island are as easy to reach as Texas, and the shading tells you where the corpus runs deep before you search.

A graph for every policy

Open any rule and the app now draws its graph straight away: the values the rule is built from, what uses it in turn, and a panel giving the entity, the unit, the period, the verification status and the citation it came from. Every node links out to its encoding, its formula and the text of the law, so you can check our work on a provision without taking our word for any of it.

•Aspen Institute State Benefits Leadership Cohort, Phoenix, October 26. Ariel and Max ran the cohort’s evening session for state benefits executives. Participants asked a chatbot real benefits questions as a resident or a caseworker and rated the answers themselves, saw on PolicyBench how the models hold up across a hundred households, and then worked in small groups on what their own teams would do with rules they can see and test. We closed on the foundation underneath all of it: rules that carry their citations and run as code can ground the AI systems states are adopting, the eligibility systems they already run, and the navigation tools families and caseworkers reach for, rather than each one reading the statute again on its own.
