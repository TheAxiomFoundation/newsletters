Feature

Ask about a benefit. See the calculation.

Our chatbot preview is open at axiom.org/chatbot. Ask about a benefit or a tax, and every dollar amount and eligibility result comes from the Axiom rules engine running rules we encoded from statutes, regulations and agency guidance, rather than from the model’s memory. Expand the tool calls under any answer to see the calculation that produced it.

It covers 34 programs today, including SNAP in 11 states and cash assistance in 17. Answers are estimates rather than determinations, 21 of those programs carry results flagged as not fully encoded, and the page says so before you type anything. Tell us when you find a wrong answer, because that is the fastest way we learn where the encodings are thin.

Benchmark snapshot
PolicyBench: the gap closed fast
PolicyBench is an open benchmark from the Axiom Foundation and PolicyEngine, scoring 46 models on United States household tax and benefit calculations. A month ago the best model missed about one household in nine; on the October 5 board it misses about one in twenty, and nine of the top ten now clear 90 percent. See the full board →

product
Product updates

The corpus · 5 October 2026
225 jurisdictions · 35,252 source documents · 357,478 provisions · 4,803 RuleSpec encodings
Every figure is live on the coverage page.
•The corpus got much broader. 225 jurisdictions, 35,252 source documents and 357,478 provisions on 5 October, against 200, 9,913 and 129,356 a month ago. PM: confirm what drove the jump — corpus bytes moving out of git into lock files, the HTS chapter adapters, ingest sessions pushing their own objects?
•Encodings held roughly steady, at 4,803. Breadth moved this month and depth did not, which is the honest shape of it. Israel is the exception, going from 13 encodings to 19 as the National Insurance employee contribution provisions landed.
•Oracles now compare concept by concept. We line up our SNAP child-support variables against the PolicyEngine variables that mean the same thing, and where January co-SNAP still differs we record it as a known difference rather than carry it quietly. One Arizona parity deviation turned out to be a dropped pin rather than the deduction we had suspected.
•The hosted API runs parity checks every day. It applies derived-rule pins, compares each parity case against its external source daily and serves the latest result, and it now says when the runtime drops a pin instead of leaving you to infer it.
•Receipt reached 0.6.3. The release closes the low findings from its architecture review and states plainly what an unpinned spec means, which matters because refusing with a named reason is the whole point of the tool.
